# Stage 1 — Capture the idea

**Status:** ✅ Decided and approved (2026-10-20)
**Output of this stage:** one chosen idea, in one sentence.

Format for each entry: *"[Who] struggles with [problem] when [situation]."*

## Chosen idea

> **Anyone actively job-hunting — student, recent graduate, or career switcher — struggles to
> know where each application stands and what is due next when the evidence is scattered
> across inboxes, portals, and spreadsheets.**

Approved by the user on 2026-10-20.

## How the "who" was defined

Deliberately **situational, not demographic**. The segment is the *situation*: multiple live
applications, real deadlines, follow-ups over a 3–6 month window, details spread across
inbox, portals, and spreadsheets. Students, recent graduates and career switchers all enter
through that same door.

Reasoning: a single demographic would have excluded users whose pain is identical. But
"everyone job-hunting" is a category, not a market — no design anchor, no first-run
experience, and a product that becomes an average of its users. The anchor is one shared
workflow; breadth is grown into, not declared.

## Idea inbox

1. **Final-year students** struggle to know the true status of each application when it is scattered across email threads, LinkedIn, and a half-updated spreadsheet.
2. **Recent graduates** struggle to not miss follow-ups and interview dates when deadlines arrive from five different inboxes.
3. **Career-services advisers** struggle to see which of their 80 students are actually making progress when they only meet each one once a term.
4. **International students** struggle to plan around visa-tied start dates when every employer's hiring timeline is different and none of them publish it.
5. **Career switchers** struggle to make their existing experience legible when every posting asks for direct experience they do not have.
6. **Students** struggle to tell whether their search is actually working when they keep no record of what they tried or what came back.

Ideas 1, 2 and 6 are absorbed by the chosen idea. Ideas 3, 4 and 5 are deferred or parked
(see below).

## Decision — how wide to define the "who"

**What the decision is:** whether the product is anchored to one demographic group or to a
shared situation. It decides what every later stage designs for.

| Option | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| A. One demographic (final-year students) | Sharpest positioning, easiest to test, no ambiguity | Arbitrary — excludes people with identical pain; undersells the product |
| B. "All job seekers" | Maximum reach on paper | No design anchor, no first-run experience, becomes an average product |
| C. Situation-defined: active multi-application search, one anchor workflow | Broad reach *and* a concrete design target; differences get parked, not blurred | Needs discipline to say "later" instead of "also"; positioning slightly less punchy |

**Decision: C.**

## Deferred and parked

| Item | Disposition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Career-switcher evidence / skills mapping | Deferred feature | Real need, but a second product; a skills-content problem, not the tracking anchor |
| Visa-tied start dates | Deferred | High stakes but narrow; we cannot source the timeline data |
| Career-services advisers | Parked | They manage *other people's* searches — different buyer, different product |
| "What did I try and what came back" | Feature of the anchor | Becomes the analytics/insight layer later, not the reason to start |
